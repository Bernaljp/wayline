### Q11. What does UMAP's sensitivity sweep cover?

- answer: n_neighbors {5,15,30,50} x min_dist {0.0,0.1,0.5} x init {spectral,pca,random} x input PCA dims {10,20,50}
- rationale: The full 108-point grid, for five reasons from the records, with one cost named at the end. 1. It is what Q1 already took. Q1 (settled by the owner) adopts the memo's direction 2 as written, and POSITION.md#Three directions spells that direction out as a sweep of "n_neighbors, min_dist, initialization (random, spectral, PCA) and input PCA dimension across seeds"; POSITION.md#Goal feasibility repeats the same four axes as "the settings that matter". Option 2 would quietly narrow a direction the owner has settled; option 1 is that direction made concrete. 2. Initialization is the objection the study has no other way to answer. The hostile reviewer's objection 5 names initialization among the things UMAP is sensitive to, and objection 7 says the finding that UMAP's global-structure advantage comes largely from initialization (Kobak and Linderman 2021, listed under "What the references lack") is not engaged. Q7 settled that no new references are absorbed for now, so the study cannot answer objection 7 by citation; varying init is the only way left to answer it, and it is the cheap way. The PAGA-initialized variant of Q10 adds one more starting point but does not separate spectral from PCA from random for plain UMAP. 3. Init bears directly on the owner's definition of Faithful (CONTEXT.md): one branch point and days in order are global and topological properties, exactly what initialization governs per objection 7, and exactly what the Q8 topology-agreement and Q17 day-order measures score. A verdict on "UMAP" that only ever ran spectral init would confound the method with its default start. 4. The PCA-dims axis is substantive for this generator, not decoration. The data is a 4-d linear latent under isotropic noise sd 0.6 across 50 genes (Q5 and Q12 evidence, data/make_cells.py:9-16), so the input PCA dimension is the knob for how much of the 46 noise dimensions reaches UMAP's kNN graph; it speaks to the memo's "easy by construction" question. And the grid contains option 2 as a sub-grid: PCA to 50 components on 50 genes is a distance-preserving rotation, so dims=50 is the no-PCA step, and umap-learn's defaults (n_neighbors 15, min_dist 0.1, spectral) are a grid point. Nothing option 2 would show is lost. 5. Compute does not argue against it. Q1's rationale says compute is no constraint, and Q6 and Q9 were settled on the same ground. 108 configurations x 20 seeds (Q9) x 2 internal splits (Q6) is 4,320 UMAP fits on 300-360 cells x at most 50 dims: seconds each, no GPU, and INTAKE.md#Budgets states no wall-clock limit. Even repeated over the ten replicate datasets of Q5 it is CPU-hours at most. The cost, so the owner can weigh it: picking the best of 108 points on two 60-cell days invites selection optimism, so the best point's split score will overstate what day 7 can reach. That does not favour option 2 (a 12-point grid has the same flaw, smaller); it is contained by Q6's two-split average, Q9's seed intervals, and by the sweep reporting the whole sensitivity surface rather than only its maximum, which is how the sweep ticket and Q14 should read it. Practical notes for the ticket: n_neighbors=50 on the 300-cell split is a sixth of the training data, large but valid and the point of that level; init='pca' is native in umap-learn 0.5+ or can be passed as an array; transform() for the scored day does not depend on init. Line check: no data or goal change (day 7 untouched, metric and command unchanged), no GPU spend, no wall-clock limit to exceed; reversed by answering Q11 again.
