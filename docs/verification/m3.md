### What the services' headers said

Crossref answered `x-rate-limit-limit: 10`, `x-rate-limit-interval: 1s`, `x-api-pool: polite-single`, which is the polite pool the `mailto` buys and matches the note's 2026-09-11 probe exactly. The seam's 0.1 s spacing is that limit.

OpenAlex answered `x-ratelimit-limit: 1000`, `x-ratelimit-limit-usd: 0.1`, `x-ratelimit-remaining-usd: 0.095`, and `x-ratelimit-cost-usd: 0` on a repeated single-entity lookup, again as the note has it. Four calls is what the whole pass spent there, which is why the chain asks OpenAlex only for what Crossref could not answer.

Unpaywall, arXiv, bioRxiv, and Europe PMC returned no rate-limit headers at all, so the seam's intervals for them come from their documentation rather than from their answers.

### What differed from the research notes

1. **Crossref's top bibliographic hit is often the wrong paper.** For "Attention Is All You Need" the best of its three rows was "Is Attention All You Need?", a different paper, at a title similarity of 0.88. The similarity check in code rejected it and the chain fell through to OpenAlex, which matched at 1.00. This is the note's "score threshold and a title similarity check in code" earning its place: Crossref's own `score` for the wrong paper was 33.2, higher than either of the other two rows. The threshold sits at 0.90.
2. **A title query can cost four requests.** The Seurat integration title resolved at Crossref, but neither Crossref nor OpenAlex carried an abstract, so Europe PMC was asked for one and Unpaywall for the PDF location. Each of those runs only because the field it fills was still empty, and this is the most expensive shape a reference has; a DOI Crossref answers in full costs one request.
3. **OpenAlex's record for a well-known paper may carry a surprising DOI and year.** Its entry for the Attention paper (`W2626778328`) now names a 2025 DOI. The record says what the source said and names the source; nothing here reconciles two services against each other.
4. **The `10.1101` prefix does not say which preprint server.** bioRxiv is asked first and medRxiv second; the SARS-CoV-2 preprint answered on the first call, so medRxiv was never asked. The API returns one entry per version, oldest first, and this preprint had three; the newest is the one the record takes, which is where its `version: 3` and its journal DOI come from.
5. **Semantic Scholar was not called once,** as the note's keyless 429 requires. A test asserts it.

### The recorded responses

`fixtures/fetch/` holds 22 responses, 692 KB, one file per request at `<host>/<request hash>.json`. The hash leaves out the `User-Agent` and the values of the `mailto` and `email` query parameters, so a recording made by one owner replays for another and no recording carries the address of whoever made it. `fixtures/fetch/README.md` is how to remake them.

## One arXiv e-print, fetched and inventoried (ticket 21, criterion 6)

`wayline absorb --inventory vaswani-2017-attention-is-all-you-need` ran on 2026-09-12 against the live `export.arxiv.org`, from the harness worktree `.claude/worktrees/ticket-21-arxiv-latex-inventory`, on Python 3.12.3 with latexpand 1.7.2, pdflatex (TeX Live 2023), and poppler 24.02. The project was laid out outside the repository and deleted afterwards: the arXiv terms of use forbid re-serving an e-print, so no part of `1706.03762` is committed anywhere in this repository, and `init` now gitignores `research/refs/*/source/` in every project.

### What the compile settled and what it did not

37 inventory items, 13 of them exact from the `.aux`, 22 still asking a reader to confirm a number. The split is the paper's own: **the Attention paper labels no equation.** Its three numbered equations are numbered 1, 2, and 3 by counting them in document order, with no page, while every figure, table, and numbered section carries a `\label` and so carries the number and the page LaTeX printed. That is why per-field `provenance` is per field: `figures`, `tables`, and `sections` say `aux`; `equations`, `algorithms`, and `theorems` say `latex`.

Two failure modes worth naming, both found here:

1. **A `\label` after a `\section*` reads as the previous section's number.** The paper ends with `\section*{Attention Visualizations}\label{sec:viz-att}`, and the `.aux` says `{7}` for that label, because that is what the section counter last reached and what a `\ref` to it prints. The first pass numbered the item 7 as well, which would have put two section 7s in one inventory. The rule now is that the compile settles a number the count guessed and never gives one to an item the document does not number; the page from the same line is still taken.
2. **A multi-row environment is one item with several numbers.** An `align` of three numbered rows is one item numbered `2-4`, and the equation after it is 5. `\nonumber`, `\notag`, and the star take a row out of the count, and `\tag` is taken as the number it prints.

### The figures

Five figures, five image files. Two are PNGs copied out of `Figures/`, three are PDFs under `vis/` rasterized with `pdftoppm -r 150`; `\graphicspath{{Figures/}}` and a `./vis/...` spelling both resolve. Without poppler the target is still recorded and the note says no image file was made, which is the degradation Implementation Decisions 9 asks for.

### What one reference costs on disk

`refs/<slug>/` held 3.0 MB: 2.5 MB of e-print source in 24 files (the tarball unpacked, plus the 48,902-character `flat.tex` the inventory's locators point into), 489 KB of figure PNGs, and a 35 KB `inventory.json`. Only the source is gitignored; the figures and the inventory are the ledger's.

A second pass sent `If-None-Match` with the stored ETag, arXiv answered 304, no bytes moved, and the kept source was re-inventoried to the same counts in 2.8 s.

## A PDF without source, and the tools venv (ticket 22, criterion 1)

The closed prototype's question: how much of the inventory does the recommended chain produce mechanically, on one PDF from the owner's field and on one arXiv e-print, counted against a by-eye count of the first ten pages, with the failure modes and the fields that are reliable per source type. The real `wayline.dossier.build_inventory` stood in for the throwaway chain, on 2026-09-12, from the harness worktree `.claude/worktrees/ticket-22-pdf-inventory`, into a project laid out in the job's scratch directory outside the repository. No PDF is committed anywhere in this repository.

### The machine and the install

`wayline tools install` built `~/.wayline/tools/` (6.9 GB) with uv 0.11.29: docling 2.126.0, pdfplumber 0.11.10, torch 2.14.0+cu126, and `device: cuda` on the RTX 3090, whose 535.309.01 driver speaks CUDA 12.2. Poppler is 24.02.0.

**The first install could not use the GPU.** The default PyPI wheel is `torch 2.14.0+cu130`, built for CUDA 13, and on a CUDA 12.2 driver `torch.cuda.is_available()` is false, so docling refused `--device cuda` outright and on the CPU took 279 s for the two-page fixture, formula enrichment included. The install now reads the driver's CUDA version from `nvidia-smi` and installs torch from the matching PyTorch index before docling (`cu126` for any 12.x driver, `cpu` without a GPU, PyPI's default for 13); the same fixture then took 28 s.

Three tools did not run and are therefore **not verified live**: GROBID, because the Docker socket refuses this user (`permission denied while trying to connect to the docker API`), so `references[]` came back `converter: none` on both PDFs; marker, whose 2.0 release reads math with a VLM behind a vLLM server in Docker; and MinerU, which was not installed. Their adapters run in the suite on samples written to each tool's documented output shape.

### The failure modes

Each of these was found on one of the three documents, is handled in code, and has a test on a document built to show it.

1. **Multi-column confusion in the text layer.** `pdftotext -layout` sets both columns on one line, and a justified left column runs to within one space of the right one ("...which is the point. The Lemma 1. Some tiny papers..."); plain `pdftotext` is no better, joining columns that share a baseline. The heading regex alone finds nothing in the second column; it is also tried where a line starts a column the page's other lines start one at, when what follows is set like a heading rather than a mention.
2. **Equation numbers split off the display.** docling put the "(2)" of an aligned pair in a text item of its own and glued "(3)" to the start of the paragraph in the next column. The number is recovered by geometry: `(N)` opening a text item level with the display.
3. **Equation numbers read into the LaTeX.** On the Nature paper the formula model spelled the printed number into its output, `\quad & ( 2 ) \\`; it is taken off the row ends. Six of that paper's eight numbers were not read at all.
4. **OCR errors in equation LaTeX.** "M u t i H e a d" for MultiHead, `d _ { \min o d l }` for $d_{model}$, every glyph spaced. Converter LaTeX is never exact form: every one is `ocr: true` and confirmed by a reader.
5. **A page footer read into a formula.** The first display of the Nature paper's page 12 ends in "( 2 0 0 ) 1:2338 | https://doi.org".
6. **One display split into two formula items** (Attention's two-line MultiHead display).
7. **Captions not linked to their figure** (the fixture's Figure 1, the Nature paper's Fig. 2): paired with the nearest captionless figure on the page and marked `confirm_fields: ["caption", ...]`.
8. **Caption splitting, or a figure cut into panels.** Attention's Figures 2 and 5 each came back as two pictures with the caption on one; an uncaptioned picture under 2% of its page joins the captioned figure on its page.
9. **Labels inside or above a figure read as headings.** "Scaled Dot-Product Attention" (the title of Figure 2's left panel) and "Input-Input Layer5" twice.
10. **A figure read as a table.** The Nature paper's Fig. 10 is a panel of numbers the table model took; its caption names it a figure, and it is inventoried as one.
11. **Badges and logos as pictures.** Two on the Nature paper's first page, one on its last; with no caption and no captioned figure on the page they are left out, and counted in a note.
12. **The title read as a heading** (the Nature paper): dropped when it repeats the PDF's own Title field.
13. **Bold run-in headings are not headings to a layout model.** Seven of the Nature paper's eight and Attention's Acknowledgements. Not fixed: nothing in docling's output marks them, so finding them is the reader pass's.
14. **A theorem and its proof as one text item; an algorithm labeled `code`** (the fixture). The heading regex still finds both; where either ends is the reader's.
15. **pdfplumber's ruled tables are not tables.** It returned the fixture's three unruled rows as one row, and counted 10 "ruled tables" in a paper with 4.
16. **Equations as images** did not occur: every display in the three documents was typeset. Nothing here measures that case.

### What is reliable, per source type, and what the reader pass fills

| Field | arXiv e-print | PDF, with docling | PDF, poppler and pdfplumber only |
|---|---|---|---|
| `page_count` | `aux`, when the source compiles | `pdfinfo`: exact | `pdfinfo`: exact |
| `text_layer` | `flat.tex`: exact | `pdftotext -layout`: exact text, columns side by side | the same |
| `raw_images` | the tarball's files: exact | `pdfimages -list`: exact | the same |
| `sections`, `appendices` | exact, numbers exact where labeled | reliable for headings on their own line (27 of 27 across both papers once figure labels are filtered); **reader**: run-in headings | **reader** |
| `equations` | LaTeX exact; numbers exact where labeled, counted otherwise | detection reliable (5 of 5 displays found, one split); **reader**: every LaTeX (OCR errors) and every number (3 of 3 on Attention, 2 of 8 on Nature) | **reader** |
| `figures` | environment, caption, and image file exact | reliable (9 of 9) with caption, number, and image file; **reader**: what each shows, a paired caption | **reader** |
| `tables` | environment and caption exact | reliable (4 of 4) with cells and captions; **reader**: numeric sanity | pdfplumber: not reliable; **reader** |
| `algorithms`, `theorems` | environments exact | heading regex finds them; **reader**: boundaries | heading regex on the text layer, by line; **reader**: boundaries |
| `references` | `.bbl`: exact | GROBID (not verified here); without it `none` and **reader** lists them | **reader** |
| `metadata` | `\title`, `\documentclass` | GROBID, else `pdfinfo` Title and Author when the PDF sets them (the Nature PDF does), else the converter's title (the Attention PDF) | `pdfinfo` |

The model pass that fills the rest is the reader of the L2 absorb, and the inventory tells it what to do: every item that needs it carries `confirm: true` and `confirm_fields` (`latex`, `number`, `boundary`, `meaning`, `caption`, `cells`), and the notes total them ("the reader pass confirms: latex on 6 item(s), meaning on 5 item(s), number on 6 item(s)" on the Attention PDF). What no field can carry, the headings a layout model does not see, is the reader pass's first job on a journal paper with run-in headings.

For an arXiv paper the e-print is the better source by every measure here: it is faster, exact where the PDF is OCR-derived, and numbered where the PDF's numbers go missing. The PDF chain is what a journal paper gets, and on one it finds the figures, tables, and standalone headings a reader would, and not the run-in headings or most equation numbers.
