# 115 - The paper order and the paper's parts

**What to build:** The paper is made only on an explicit order, and only from the outline and the sections. The owner says so in a conversation and confirms a card. The card carries a readiness list that code builds at no cost:

- open objectives;
- the outline's gaps;
- the general report's last kill argument and its standing points;
- results changed since their last drawing;
- inherited claims unchecked or unverifiable;
- open contradictions;
- unresolved references;
- concerns for the order;
- the outline's link.

The card also gives the cost (about $35 to $40 for six figures when the first build passes, about $10 more per failed build). Nothing blocks the owner's order. An order with nothing verified is allowed: its batch then asks whether to keep the unchecked inherited results as stated (the default) or leave them out, and `PAPER.md` says none was checked. `wayline paper` stays the session's tool and the dashboard's Confirm runs it. No decision ticket opens.

The order is a rest point. Running tickets finish, nothing new launches, changed results are drawn, the paper is composed from that commit, and the run resumes afterward if it had not ended. The order starts no research ticket; a gate point that needs evidence is a proposed objective.

Under `interactive` and `checkpoints` one batch is asked before any model writes, through 79's record. It holds the open contradictions (each with code's suggested side or "a typo, fix it"), the unverifiable claims the owner might keep, the unresolved references (hand over, drop, or drop the reference but keep the claim marked "to be resolved"), and the outline's open proposals. Each item has code's default, and "take all defaults" is one answer. This ticket builds the batch with those items. The figure plan and oversized figures join it in 116.

One writer session on `opus xhigh` then composes the whole paper from the outline and the sections, each part its own field. Code writes `research/paper/`:

- the parts: `title.md`, `abstract.md`, `introduction.md`, `results.md`, `discussion.md`, `methods.md`, `supplement.md`, and `front.md`, each heading carrying its outline id as a pandoc attribute (`## ... {#R3}`);
- `tables/T1.csv` with `T1.md`;
- `references.json`;
- `alternatives/<part>-<n>.md`;
- the build report `PAPER.md`: the commit, each part with its version, the batch's answers, what was left out and why, the owner's passages whose wording changed, and the cost.

A Type-A check compares the paper's headings with the outline's order and placement, and `check_structure` also checks order on a venue that is not `structure_flexible`. A change the writer wants is a proposal in its notes, never a move. Inherited title, abstract, and captions never reach the paper, and unchecked framing is built on, never copied. Code writes the data and code availability statements from the records. Authors, affiliations, acknowledgments, funding, and competing interests are one setup question at every autonomy, left as marked placeholders until said.

Each order makes a new version, with the previous one frozen under `research/paper/earlier/v<n>/`. A re-order recomposes only the parts whose inputs changed (title and abstract whenever a main-text part did) and keeps an owner's edit of a part file unless its inputs changed. The writer and the gates always see the whole version. The writer offers three titles; other alternatives come only on the owner's ask or a gate point with two honest fixes. The owner chooses.

Today's `paper.md`, `build.json`, `--continue`, and the gate's two `writing` tickets on `research/paper/paper.md` go, and the writer no longer lists references. BRIEF P7 Report retires, P8 becomes Paper order, and R26 reads "The general report is always current; the paper is made only on an explicit order, from the owner, or from the decider in an autonomous run."
